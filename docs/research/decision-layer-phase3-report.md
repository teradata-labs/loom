# Decision layer, Phase 3: shadow evidence for the six live-path sites

**Date**: 2026-09-23 · **Branch**: `feat/decision-layer-phase3` (PR #410, stacked on #409) · **Status**: ⚠️ first campaign; no band recommended yet

This report records what the six Phase 3 sites saw when a real decider (Jev, through the Vercel AI Gateway) ran in shadow next to Loom's existing mechanisms on the gauntlet rig. It is evidence for choosing bands, not a claim that any band is right. Every "agreement" figure below is agreement with the mechanism Loom runs today, which is the reference but not the truth; where the two disagree, only a labelled sample can settle it.

## 1. Setup

- **Rig**: the Azure gauntlet VM (`loom-mcp-rg`), `looms serve` from this branch, Azure OpenAI gpt-4o for every generative call, the teradata MCP server for tools.
- **Decider**: Jev through the gateway (`typesafe-ai/jev`), `requests_per_minute: 26` against the free tier's 30, shared by every agent in a process (`jev.Shared`). Shadow only: no `bands` configured anywhere.
- **Agents** (`~/decision-campaign/agents`, generated by `gen_campaign.py`): one Teradata analyst with graph memory and extraction on (`dl-analyst`), a one-word classifier (`dl-classifier`), a worker (`dl-worker`), two leaning voters (`dl-voter-a/b`), two fixed-side voters (`dl-voter-c/d`), two debaters (`dl-debater-a/b`), and a judge that also moderates debates (`dl-judge`). Prompts are task instructions, not personas.
- **Traffic**, run sequentially so two processes never exceed the gateway budget:
  - 25 chat sessions × 4 turns on `dl-analyst`: a turn stating team facts, a bounded database question, a recall question ("what did I tell you about my team?"), a second database question. Exercises `recall.rerank`, `tool_search.rerank` and `tool.failure_kind`.
  - 40 conditional workflows (bug / feature / question / default over issue reports) → `workflow.branch`.
  - 20 pipelines, one stage each with a `validation_prompt` and one retry → `stage.validation`.
  - 20 swarms with the leaning voters and 20 with the fixed-side voters, judge configured → `swarm.tie_break`.
  - 15 two-round debates with the judge as moderator → `debate.consensus`.
- **Rows**: `decision_shadow` in the rig's `loom.db`, read with `status.sh`, `final-report.sh` and `loom decision report --site`.

## 2. What the campaign itself found (bugs fixed before the numbers count)

Running real traffic against the layer surfaced five defects, all fixed on the branch during the campaign and all of a kind a unit test would not have caught:

1. `looms serve` builds agents on two paths of its own (startup from YAML and hot-reload) and neither passed the decision config; only registry-built agents ever had a decider. The layer had been "configured" on the rig with nothing behind it.
2. `looms workflow run` opened no shadow store and exited before its agents' background shadows were recorded. Workflow-site rows were traced but never persisted.
3. The workflow CLI did not run storage migrations; a binary ahead of the schema had every insert rejected, and the recorder only counted the failures. 37 conditional runs left no rows and were rerun.
4. ERROR-path rows carried no reason. The record now has an `error` field (proto, both stores, report), and the shadow goroutine logs decider errors at Warn.
5. The Postgres store's tenant isolation relied on RLS alone; RLS does not bind the table owner without FORCE and never binds a superuser. Found by CI on #409, reproduced against Postgres 16, fixed with FORCE and an explicit `user_id` predicate.

Two gateway facts also came out: the free tier returns an intermittent HTTP 503 on the largest rerank requests (28 of 32 64-candidate requests succeeded; smaller ones never failed), and the client's 200 ms retry base spent all three attempts inside the outage window. The base is now 500 ms, capped at 4 s.

## 4. Reading the results

**Failure kind is the strongest candidate.** 95.5% agreement, and agreement rises with confidence (100% in the 0.7–0.9 bin). The 14 disagreements are the reference's `other` bucket again (Jev says `bad_input`, five rows) plus a few `not_found` rows Jev reads as `auth` or `transient`. Same picture as Phases 1 and 2, now on live agent traffic rather than replayed rows.

**Branch routing and stage validation look good on small samples.** Reading all 40 conditional outputs, the classifier put every issue on a defensible branch and never fell through to the default; Jev disagreed on 4 of 42, all at low confidence (100% agreement at 0.7–0.9). Stage validation agreed on 19 of 20; the one miss (pipeline 2, "at most sixty words", 52 words) was Jev saying invalid at 0.30 confidence, so a band at any sensible threshold would not have acted on it. Both need more rows before a band.

**Debate consensus: Jev was right on every real disagreement.** Reading the debate outputs, 11 of 15 debates had both debaters take the same side (see §5 on why), and in those the heuristic's "consensus" is trivially right and Jev agreed. The three where Jev said "no consensus" (debates 4, 9 and 15) are exactly the three where the positions genuinely opposed each other; the heuristic called all three "consensus" because both debaters were confident. So the 80% figure understates it: by inspection Jev scored 15 of 15 and the heuristic 12 of 15. This is the site where the reference is known to be weak, and the evidence is that the decider fixes it.

**Recall rerank: a one-directional disagreement, not noise.** Of 4,693 candidates, both parties agreed on 2,216 irrelevant and 281 relevant. Jev then kept 1,502 more that the LLM dropped, at 0.80 mean confidence, and dropped only 50 that the LLM kept. Agreement is *lowest* in Jev's high-confidence bins, so this is not a calibration artefact; Jev is confidently more generous. In this campaign the memory store filled with the same handful of team facts repeated across 25 sessions, so many of those extra memories were genuinely about the team and the LLM (which answers with a handful of indexes) is the stricter party. Which is better for the answer is a product question. No band without a labelled sample.

**Tool search rerank: the reference was wrong, not the decider.** The LLM reranker re-orders candidates and returns everything it scores above 0.3; for these ten queries it returned all of them, so the reference read "relevant" for every candidate and Jev, which marked one or two per query relevant, scored 12.7%. The reference is now the LLM's score at or above 0.5 (the same threshold as the Noul), which is a relevance verdict rather than a return list. These 134 rows should be read as "Jev is far more selective than a reranker that returns everything", and rerun under the corrected reference.

**Swarm tie-break, after the fix: Jev mostly abstains.** With the stance fix in place every one of 20 swarms tied and reached the judge. Over the 26 tie-breaks recorded after the fixes, Jev answered `none_of_these` 16 times ("the reasons do not favour either tied choice"), agreed with the judge 6 times and disagreed 4 times. When two voters argue assigned sides honestly the evidence is balanced by construction, so an abstention is a defensible reading; the judge, a generative agent asked to pick, always picks. In live mode `none_of_these` falls back to the judge, so this site would skip the judge's turn in roughly four ties in ten. Small sample, and the reference here is one agent's opinion.

**Debate consensus, after the fix: 15 of 15 by inspection.** Rerun on the 15 debates with the debaters holding their stances, 14 had genuinely opposing positions and one had both debaters agreeing. Jev said "no consensus" on exactly the 14 and "consensus" on the one. The confidence-mean heuristic said "consensus" on all 15, because both debaters were confident every time. Agreement with the reference is therefore 1 of 15; agreement with the positions as written is 15 of 15. This is the clearest result in the campaign: the heuristic does not measure consensus at all, and the decider does.

## 5. What the workflow outputs themselves showed

Reading the outputs, not just the shadow rows, found things about Loom's workflows that the rows could not:

- **Cost was under-reported everywhere.** Debates reported $0 and 0 LLM calls (the orchestrator never filled the cost). Conditionals counted only the branch, not the classifier turn or its retries. Pipelines counted stages, not validation calls. Swarms counted votes, not the judge. All four are fixed on this branch, with regression tests, and `AgentPosition` now carries its usage.
- **Debaters and voters ignored their configured stance, and the cause was not the prompt.** Asked directly through the server, `dl-debater-b` argues against the proposition and `dl-voter-d` votes for the second option. Through `looms workflow run`, in a plain fork-join with the identical prompt, both ignored their instructions. Root cause: `WithSystemPrompt` writes into the agent's `Config`, and `registry.buildAgent` (the path the workflow CLI and dynamic creation use) then applied `WithConfig` with a fresh `Config`, replacing the struct and dropping the prompt. **Every registry-built agent ran without its system prompt.** `looms serve` was unaffected only because its own `Config` carries the prompt explicitly. Fixed: `WithConfig` keeps name, description and system prompt from earlier options when the incoming config leaves them empty, and `buildAgent` carries them explicitly; regression tests at both levels.
- **The orchestrator prompts also eroded stance, and were fixed first.** The debate injected a perspective guessed from the agent id's suffix (a hardcoded map) or "approach this from your unique angle" plus "consider alternative perspectives"; the swarm opened with "You are participating in a swarm voting process" (a role prompt, project rule 8) and framed the task as evaluating the question. Both now say: take the standpoint set out in your own instructions and hold it. On their own these changes moved the swarm from 5 to 6 ties out of 20 and the debates not at all, which is what pointed at the CLI.
- **Verification after both fixes, same 15 debates and 20 swarms, same binary path.** Debates with genuinely opposing positions: 4 of 15 → **14 of 15** (the fifteenth had both debaters agreeing that rewrites are usually a mistake). Swarms reaching the judge: 5 of 20 → **20 of 20**.
- **The stage validator is lenient about "only".** Four pipelines asked for "only the SQL / JSON / CSV / command" and got fenced code blocks; the LLM validator passed all four, and so did Jev. Neither reads "only" strictly. If that matters, the criterion has to say "no code fences".

## 6. What this does and does not support

- **Supports** planning the first live band on `tool.failure_kind`, on the Choice question, after a labelled sample of a few hundred of its `other`-bucket disagreements. The Noul question (`retry_same_input_helps`) is nearly always "false" on both sides and needs failures that *are* retryable in the sample before it says anything.
- **Supports** replacing the consensus heuristic with `debate.consensus` even though it adds a call: on 14 genuinely opposed debates the heuristic declared consensus 14 times and the decider 0 times, and both agreed on the one debate where the debaters actually agreed. The heuristic measures confidence, not agreement; the sample is small but the mechanism is fully understood.
- **Supports** treating the stance bug as the more important find of the campaign: until this branch, every agent built by `looms workflow run` or created dynamically ran without its system prompt.
- **Supports** continuing to collect `workflow.branch` and `stage.validation` rows; the signal is good and the calibration tracks, but 42 and 20 rows are not a basis for a band.
- **Does not support** a band on `recall.rerank`. 61.6% agreement, with agreement falling as confidence rises, is a substantive disagreement about what "relevant" means, and only labels settle it.
- **Does not support** any conclusion about `tool_search.rerank` (wrong reference, now corrected). `swarm.tie_break` has 26 usable rows after the fix and a decider that abstains on most balanced ties; not a band candidate yet.
- **Does not support** any claim that Jev is right. Every figure here is agreement with what Loom does today, except where the outputs were read by hand (debates, conditionals).

Nothing is switched on. Every site remains in shadow.

## 7. LongMemEval A/B for `recall.rerank` (in flight)

Cell design: A = today's LLM rerank with Jev in shadow (`longmemeval` agent), B = Jev live on `recall.rerank` (`longmemeval-jev`, band `act_min 0.5`, `per_question`, `replace`); multi-session and knowledge-update questions, N=40 each, isolate mode, gpt-4o server on the LongMemEval VM, one judge for both. Two readouts: end-to-end accuracy per cell (official metric) and, the primary one, per-decision grading: every recall shadow row carries `subject = session:<loom session>` for the memory it judged, `EntryResult.haystack_sessions` maps those to the dataset's evidence flags, and `grade_recall.py` reports precision/recall of "keep" for Jev and for the LLM rerank against the same truth.

**Found before the numbers count.** The first launch of cell A recalled, for every question, memories that had no session provenance and predated the run. Cause: isolate-mode temp agents were named `lme-tmp-<question id>`, graph memory is scoped by agent name, and `DeleteAgent` does not purge graph memory, so a rerun of a question recalled everything earlier runs (the August 4×500 matrix included) had extracted for it. Every earlier isolate-mode run on this VM shared that flaw; it duplicates the retrieval pool rather than changing its content, so it does not by itself invalidate earlier accuracy figures, but it made per-decision grading impossible (no provenance) and it is not the experiment described. Fixed 2026-09-23: temp agent names carry a per-run nonce, and memories the agent writes through the `graph_memory` tool record their session too (`source: agent`, `source_id: <session>`). Cell A relaunched at 19:55Z on the rebuilt server and runner; the partial first attempt is kept as `out/A-multi-session.*.stale-scope`. Results follow when the four cells finish (~15 h).

**Second finding before the numbers count.** Every recall shadow row from a live turn had an empty `session_id`: `decision.SessionIDFromContext` read only its own context key, while the conversation loop sets the agent session with `pkg/session`. Fixed in the decision package (fallback to the agent session; regression tests on the recall path). Cell A multi-session was graded anyway by attributing each rerank visit to the entry whose haystack sessions its subjects name and taking the last visit per entry as the question turn (`grade_recall_by_subject.py`); later cells join on `session_id` directly (`grade_recall.py`). Cell A knowledge-update was restarted on the fixed server.

**Cell A multi-session, first readout (2026-09-24 00:20Z; 37 of 40 entries answered, 3 lost to Azure OpenAI's content filter on a haystack session; 31 question turns graded, 6 lost to gateway 503s on the question turn; 1,061 keep-or-drop decisions).** End-to-end: 20/37 (54.1%) under the gpt-5.2 judge, on the same server whose earlier isolate-mode multi-session runs scored 9% with the hand-built agent config that ignored `--agent`. Per decision, against the dataset's evidence sessions:

| Mechanism | Kept evidence | Kept non-evidence | Dropped evidence | Dropped non-evidence | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jev (shadow) | 190 | 10 | 149 | 712 | 95.0% | 56.0% |
| LLM rerank (acted) | 137 | 3 | 202 | 719 | 97.9% | 40.4% |

Both kept at least one evidence memory in 30 of the 31 questions; Jev kept 6.5 of 34 candidates on average, the LLM rerank 4.5. Read: the 61.6% "agreement" from the rig campaign was Jev disagreeing with a reference that drops evidence. Jev keeps 39% more evidence memories at a cost of 7 extra non-evidence memories per 1,000 decisions. Whether that turns into answers is what cell B measures; a rerank can only surface what retrieval fetched, and here every graded question had evidence among the candidates.

**Why requests failed, and the fix.** Of the failed recall requests, 364 of 390 were the upstream's 503 "Service temporarily unavailable", 22 our own 3 s live cap, 4 a 429 that said the upstream was busy (not our key's rate). Failure rate by request size: 1–10 questions 0%, 11–20 1%, 21–40 9%, 41–50 27%, 51–64 39%; failed requests also took longer (median 2.1 s vs 0.9 s). The bulk of a rerank request is the candidate list in state (about 7,000 tokens at 64 candidates), so the fix carves state with questions: `decision.Chunk` splits a request into concurrent chunks (Jev hint 16 questions), each carrying only its own candidates (`fan_out_key`), and bisects any chunk that still overloads. Deployed 2026-09-24 and cell B restarted on it, so B's Jev acts on nearly every visit rather than falling back on a third of them.
